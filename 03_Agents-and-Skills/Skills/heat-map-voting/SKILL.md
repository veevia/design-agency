# Heat Map Voting

**TL;DR:** Dot stickers (or digital equivalents) placed directly on sketch elements reveal where the team's collective attention lands — without anyone having to justify a position publicly first. Clusters of dots show the team what's resonating; lone dots reveal hidden gems that might be missed in verbal discussion.

## When to use

- During or immediately after the Art Museum gallery walk
- As a pre-discussion signal before Speed Critique
- Whenever you need to surface team preferences without social pressure from verbal debate

## Materials

- Small dot stickers (2–3 per person) OR digital dot stamps
- All sketches posted on the wall/board

## The rules

- **Equal dots per person:** Everyone gets the same number — typically 2–3 dots. No exceptions, even for the Decider.
- **Place on elements, not on whole sketches:** A dot goes on a specific panel, feature, or annotation — not on "the sketch as a whole." This identifies which parts are compelling, not just which sketch "won."
- **Silent:** No explaining where you put your dot. No lobbying others.
- **Dot placement is not the final decision:** Heat map shows interest; Supervote makes the decision.

## Steps

1. **Distribute dots (1 min):** Give each person 2–3 small dot stickers (or digital equivalents).
2. **Dot placement (5–7 min, silent):** Each person walks the sketches and places dots on specific elements they find interesting, compelling, or important. Can split dots across different sketches.
3. **Step back (1 min):** Everyone steps back and views the full heat map.
4. **Observation (2 min):** Note clusters (clear interest), isolated dots (hidden gems), and blank areas (elements that didn't land).
5. **Feed into Speed Critique:** The Speed Critique focuses discussion on the dotted areas — not the entire sketch.

## Reading the heat map

| Pattern | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Dense cluster on one panel | This interaction/feature has strong team resonance — discuss first |
| Spread across multiple sketches | Team sees value in different approaches — combination may be possible |
| Isolated dot (1 person only) | May be a hidden insight worth naming in Speed Critique |
| No dots on a sketch | Either unconvincing or too similar to another sketch — name it |
| Dots on annotations, not visuals | Team is responding to the *idea* more than the *UI* |

## Digital adaptation

On a shared whiteboard:
- Use the board's dot stickers or stamp tool
- Assign each participant a colour dot so you can see who voted where
- Timer: 7 min; no verbal communication
- At time: zoom out to see the full heat map

## Agent facilitation guide

When producing a heat map analysis from a set of solution sketches:
1. Review all sketches against the sprint target
2. For each sketch, identify the 2–3 elements most directly addressing Sprint Questions
3. Generate a synthetic "heat map" by weighting: target-alignment × novelty × specificity
4. Flag the top 3 elements across all sketches as "must-discuss in Speed Critique"
5. Flag any sketch that received no interest — not to dismiss it, but to name why it's not resonating

Output:
```markdown
## Heat Map Summary — [Sprint Name]

| Sketch | Dotted element | Dot count | Sprint Q addressed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reasons-First Triage | Urgency score display | ●●●● | Q2 |
| Reasons-First Triage | Inline records list | ●●● | Q2 |
| One-Tap Assign | One-tap specialist assignment | ●● | Q3 |
| Shared Checking | Multi-coordinator "confirmed by" trail | ● | Q2 |

**Clusters:** Urgency scoring + record visibility (Reasons-First Triage sketch)
**Hidden gem:** Multi-coordinator "confirmed by" trail (1 dot — not obvious, worth discussing)
**Blank:** Checklist panel in Checking Flow sketch — no interest
```

## Watch-outs

- **Dots on whole sketches:** "I put my dot on this sketch" — too broad. Redirect: "Which specific element are you interested in?"
- **Skipping heat map, going straight to discussion:** Verbal discussion without heat map data biases toward the loudest voice. Always dot first.
- **Decider placing dots before others:** The Decider's dots shouldn't be visible until others have placed theirs. In digital: reveal simultaneously.
