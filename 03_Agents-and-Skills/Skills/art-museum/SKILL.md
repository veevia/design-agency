# Art Museum

**TL;DR:** Post all solution sketches on the wall (or in a shared digital board), give everyone time to walk around and read in silence, then proceed to structured critique. The "gallery walk" format ensures every team member forms their own opinion before group discussion — preventing the first voice in the room from setting the frame for everyone else.

## When to use

- Immediately after all Solution Sketches are complete
- Before any discussion, voting, or critique
- Whenever you need independent evaluation before group convergence

## Materials

- Wall space or a shared digital whiteboard
- All completed Solution Sketches (anonymous — no names attached)
- Sticky dots for Heat Map voting (next method)
- Timer: 10–15 min

## Steps

1. **Post all sketches (5 min):** Hang or upload all sketches in a row — equally spaced, no clustering by creator. Anonymous: no names, no labels identifying who made what.
2. **Silent walk-through (10–15 min):** Every team member walks the wall independently. No discussion. Observers may take notes on paper but do not speak.
3. **Optional: mark interesting elements (parallel to the walk):** While reading, team members can begin placing dot stickers on interesting elements — this flows directly into [Heat Map Voting](../heat-map-voting/SKILL.md).
4. **Transition to critique:** When time is up, proceed to Speed Critique — first structured discussion about what was seen.

## Why silence matters

The Art Museum's power is in the silence. When people review independently:
- They form opinions based on the work, not the presenter's authority
- Junior team members vote the same as senior ones
- Ideas are evaluated on merit, not on who proposed them
- The Decider's opinion doesn't set the frame before others have theirs

Breaking the silence before everyone has reviewed defeats the purpose. Enforce it even if it feels awkward.

## Arrangement principles

| Principle | Why |
|---|---|
| Equally spaced | Prevents visual clustering that suggests grouping/priority |
| Anonymous | No creator label until after straw poll |
| Same size | Sketches printed/displayed at the same scale so visual impact isn't from paper size |
| Readable distance | If on a wall, large enough to read from 1m away |

## Digital adaptation

In remote sprints (on a shared whiteboard):
- Upload all sketches as images to a "Gallery" frame
- Each sketch gets its own labelled area (Concept A, B, C…)
- Team members join the board and zoom around independently
- Use a 10-min timer; no verbal communication in the call during review
- Use emoji reactions or dot stamps for Heat Map equivalent

## Agent facilitation guide

When reviewing a set of solution sketches as an agent:
1. Review each sketch independently before comparing
2. For each sketch: identify the core mechanism, the target moment it addresses, and the key UX claim
3. Flag any sketches that do not address the sprint target moment (off-target)
4. Prepare a one-sentence summary per sketch for Speed Critique
5. Identify which sketches are most different from each other (these are the candidates worth comparing in Straw Poll)

## Watch-outs

- **Someone explains their sketch while others are still reading:** Halt immediately. "We're still in gallery walk — let's give everyone the full review time."
- **Sketches posted with creator names:** Strip names before posting. Re-anonymise if needed.
- **Short-changing the review time:** 10 minutes feels long. It isn't. Complex sketches take time to understand. Don't rush to critique.
- **Skipping to "I love this one":** Enthusiasm before structure biases the group. Reserve all preference signals for Heat Map and Straw Poll.
