# Individual Ideation (Notes and Ideas)

**TL;DR:** Before anyone picks up a marker to sketch, they spend 15–20 minutes alone with their notes. This is not passive re-reading — it's active idea capture. Each person walks through everything they know about the sprint problem, jots down concepts they want to explore, screenshots or writes references to analogous products that inspired them, and sketches messy first thoughts. The goal is to activate each person's individual knowledge *before* the group's shared mental model takes over.

This step sits between Lightning Demos and Crazy 8s. It is the transition from inspiration to generative thinking — the individual equivalent of warming up before a sprint.

## When to use

- After Lightning Demos, before Crazy 8s
- When the team has absorbed a large amount of information (talks, interviews, research) and needs to process it individually before producing
- Whenever you want sketches to reflect diverse individual thinking, not a group convergence
- In remote sprints where Crazy 8s can otherwise become too heavily anchored to the first ideas shared in the group

## What "notes and ideas" means in practice

This is not formal documentation. It's personal, messy, and disposable. Each person might:

- Write a one-line description of a concept they want to explore ("what if the queue showed urgency scores inline?")
- Jot a reference to a Lightning Demo they found inspiring ("the ride-hailing live-tracking pattern from Demo 3")
- Sketch a rough 2-box diagram of an interaction they're imagining
- List the Sprint Questions that feel most central to them personally
- Write down "I don't know how this would work, but..." — those are worth capturing too

The output of this step is not shared with the group. It is personal input material for Crazy 8s and the Solution Sketch.

## Materials

- Individual notebook or blank paper (not a shared board)
- Pen or pencil — no markers (this is not a sketch; it's notes)
- Sprint Questions posted visibly on the wall
- Reference materials from Lightning Demos available to look back at
- Timer: 15–20 min

## Steps

1. **Set the frame (2 min):** Facilitator says: "Take 15 minutes for yourself. Look through your notes from the last day or two. Write down any ideas you want to explore. Don't worry about whether they're good — capture them. This is just for you."

2. **Individual silent work (15 min):** No talking. Each person works alone. The facilitator does not interrupt.

3. **Pick one idea to develop (2 min):** At the end, each person identifies the one concept from their notes that most directly addresses the Sprint Questions. This becomes their Crazy 8s anchor.

4. **Move into Crazy 8s immediately:** No sharing of notes between team members. Crazy 8s proceed with each person working from their own idea — diversity is preserved.

## What to look for in your notes

| What to find | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **The idea you keep coming back to** | This is your strongest intuition — worth exploring in Crazy 8s |
| **A reference from Lightning Demos that stuck** | Analogous solutions are most powerful when they're remembered, not forced |
| **A Sprint Question you think you know the answer to** | Sketch that answer — the team can challenge it |
| **An idea that feels "too obvious"** | Often it's obvious because it's right — sketch it anyway |
| **The idea you're afraid to sketch** | These are often the most interesting — they're high-risk but high-signal |

## Connection to the sketching sequence

```
Lightning Demos (external inspiration)
        ↓
Individual Ideation — Notes and Ideas (personal synthesis)
        ↓
Crazy 8s (rapid divergent sketching from personal anchor idea)
        ↓
Solution Sketch (refined, detailed 3-panel concept)
```

Each step moves from external to internal, from broad to specific, from group to individual. Individual Ideation is the pivot point — it's where external inspiration becomes personal synthesis.

## Agent facilitation guide

When supporting a team through Individual Ideation:
1. Remind participants of the top 2–3 Sprint Questions — these are the frame for idea capture
2. Suggest 3 starting prompts if participants feel stuck:
   - "What's the most direct way to address Sprint Question 1?"
   - "Which Lightning Demo element could be adapted for this product?"
   - "What would a 10x better version of the user's current workaround look like?"
3. After 15 minutes, prompt participants to identify their one strongest concept
4. Do not collect or share individual notes — they feed directly into Crazy 8s

## Watch-outs

- **Skipping this step:** Teams that go straight from Lightning Demos to Crazy 8s often produce homogeneous sketches anchored on the most recent demo. Individual Ideation breaks that groupthink by inserting a personal synthesis step.
- **Turning it into a group discussion:** "Has anyone thought about X?" during Individual Ideation contaminates the independence. Enforce silence.
- **Writing instead of thinking:** Some people use this time to write polished notes rather than capture raw ideas. Redirect: "Write fast, write messy — we're not making a document."
- **Forcing sharing:** The individual notes are not shared. Some participants want to show their ideas to get feedback before Crazy 8s. Defer: "Keep it to yourself for now — you'll share the sketch."
